# 8.4 Compound Interest

## Overview

**Compound Interest (CI)** is interest calculated on the initial principal *and* on the accumulated interest from previous periods. Unlike Simple Interest, where the interest base stays constant, Compound Interest makes your money grow exponentially -- "interest on interest." It is one of the most frequently tested topics in banking exams (SBI PO, IBPS, RBI), SSC, CAT, and placement aptitude rounds.

Understanding CI thoroughly also unlocks problems on population growth, depreciation of assets, and effective rates of return -- all of which appear regularly in competitive exams.

## One-Line Formula Cheat Sheet

```
Amount (Annual)         A = P (1 + R/100)^T
Compound Interest       CI = A - P  =  P [(1 + R/100)^T - 1]
Half-Yearly Comp.       A = P (1 + R/200)^(2T)
Quarterly Comp.         A = P (1 + R/400)^(4T)
Monthly Comp.           A = P (1 + R/1200)^(12T)
CI - SI (2 years)       Difference = P (R/100)^2
CI - SI (3 years)       Difference = P (R/100)^2 (3 + R/100)
Population Growth       P_after = P_now (1 + R/100)^T
Depreciation            V_after = V_now (1 - R/100)^T
Effective Annual Rate   E = (1 + R/n)^n - 1   (n = compounding periods/year)
```

## Real-World Applications

- **Banking:** Home loans, car loans, credit card interest are all compounded
- **Investments:** Fixed deposits, mutual funds, SIPs grow via compounding
- **Population Studies:** Population growth/decline follows the compounding model
- **Asset Valuation:** Depreciation of machinery, vehicles uses the CI depreciation formula
- **Inflation:** Price escalation over years follows a compounding pattern
