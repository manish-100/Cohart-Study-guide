# 8.6 Number System

## Overview

The **Number System** is the backbone of quantitative aptitude. Every arithmetic operation, every algebra problem, and nearly every reasoning puzzle ultimately rests on how numbers behave. This topic covers the classification of numbers, divisibility, prime factorization, factors and multiples, remainders, unit digit cycles, and special number properties. It is a high-frequency topic in exams like SSC, Banking, CAT, GATE, and all state-level competitive tests.

Mastering the number system gives you a decisive edge: it speeds up mental calculations, unlocks shortcuts in other topics, and builds the numerical intuition that examiners love to test.

## One-Line Formula Cheat Sheet

```
Number of factors of N = (a+1)(b+1)(c+1)...     where N = p^a * q^b * r^c ...
Sum of factors         = [(p^(a+1)-1)/(p-1)] * [(q^(b+1)-1)/(q-1)] * ...
Number of even factors = a * (b+1)(c+1)...       (subtract 1 from exponent of 2)
Number of odd factors  = (b+1)(c+1)...           (ignore the factor of 2 entirely)
Product of all factors = N^(d/2)                 where d = total number of factors
Sum of co-primes < N   = N * phi(N) / 2         where phi(N) = Euler's totient
Remainder of a^n / d   = use cyclicity of unit digits or Fermat/Euler theorem
Divisibility by 2      = last digit even
Divisibility by 3      = sum of digits divisible by 3
Divisibility by 4      = last two digits divisible by 4
Divisibility by 5      = last digit 0 or 5
Divisibility by 6      = divisible by both 2 and 3
Divisibility by 8      = last three digits divisible by 8
Divisibility by 9      = sum of digits divisible by 9
Divisibility by 11     = (sum of odd-place digits) - (sum of even-place digits) is 0 or divisible by 11
```
