## Detailed Explanations

### Q1. (c) Both follow.
All cats are dogs + All dogs are birds = All cats are birds (I follows). Conversion: Some birds are cats (II follows).

### Q2. (b) Only II follows.
No tree is flower + All flowers are fruits: can't conclude about trees and fruits definitely. But All flowers are fruits -> Some fruits are flowers. Since no tree is flower, the flowers-part of fruits is tree-free. Trees could still overlap with non-flower fruits. "Some fruits are not trees" -- since flowers are fruits and not trees, there exist fruits (flowers) that are not trees. II follows. "No tree is fruit" fails because trees might be fruits through a different path.

### Q3. (c) Either I or II follows.
Some+Some = no definite conclusion. I (Some pens are books) and II (No pen is book) are complementary (I/E pair).

### Q4. (c) Both follow.
All students are scholars + No scholar is lazy = No student is lazy (I). All students are scholars -> Some scholars are students (II).

### Q5. (a) Only I follows.
Some apples are oranges + All oranges are mangoes = Some apples are mangoes (I). "All mangoes are oranges" -- reverse of P2, not valid (II fails).

### Q6. (b) Only II follows.
All cars are buses + Some buses are trains: All+Some = no definite conclusion about cars and trains. Some buses are trains -> Some trains are buses (conversion, II follows).

### Q7. (d) Neither follows.
No+No = No definite conclusion. No man is boy + No boy is girl: Men and girls may or may not overlap.

### Q8. (a) Only I follows.
Some B are C + No C is D -> Some B are not D (I follows). All A are B + Some B are C: no definite A-C conclusion (II fails).

### Q9. (a) Only I follows.
Some+All = Some kings are princesses (I). "All princesses are queens" is reverse, not valid (II fails).

### Q10. (c) Both follow.
All+All chain. I: All roses are beautiful. II: Some beautiful are red (conversion of All red are beautiful).

### Q11. (b) Only II follows.
No chair is table + All tables are wood: chairs and wood may overlap (I fails). But tables are wood and not chairs, so some wood is not chair (II follows).

### Q12. (a) Only I follows.
Some M are N + All N are O = Some M are O (I). Some O are P is separate; Some+Some for M-P gives no conclusion (II fails).

### Q13. (d) Neither follows.
All doctors are engineers + Some engineers are teachers: All+Some = no definite conclusion.

### Q14. (a) Only I follows.
No X is Y + Some Y are Z = Some Z are not X (I). "No X is Z" -- X might overlap with Z outside Y (II fails).

### Q15. (a) Only I follows.
All+No: No river is ocean (I). "Some oceans are rivers" contradicts this (II fails).

### Q16. (c) Either I or II follows.
Some+Some = no definite conclusion. I and II are I/E complementary pair.

### Q17. (d) All follow.
All+All+All chain. All cups are bowls (I), All saucers are bowls (II), Some bowls are cups (III -- conversion).

### Q18. (a) Only I follows.
Some+All: Some birds are fish (I). "All fish are birds" is not valid (II fails).

### Q19. (c) Both follow.
All+No: No politician is corrupt (I). All politicians are honest -> Some honest are politicians (II).

### Q20. (d) All follow.
Some+All: Some books are erasers (I). All+No: No pen is sharpener (II). From I + No eraser is sharpener: Some books are not sharpeners (III).

### Q21. (a) Only I follows.
All+No: No singer is painter (I). "Some painters are singers" contradicts this (II fails).

### Q22. (c) Either I or II follows.
Some+Some = no conclusion. I (All phones are tablets) and II (Some phones are not tablets) are A/O complementary.

### Q23. (c) All follow.
All X are Y + No Y is Z = No X is Z (I). Some Z are W -> Some W are Z (III). No Y is Z + Some Z are W: those W that are Z are not Y -> Some W are not Y (II).

### Q24. (a) Only I follows.
Some+All: Some girls are natural (I). "All natural are beautiful" is reverse (II fails).

### Q25. (a) Only I follows.
No A is B + All B are C: B is in C but not in A. Some C (the B part) are not A (I). "No A is C" fails -- A might overlap non-B part of C.

### Q26. (a) Only I follows.
Some tall are rivers + All rivers are deep = Some tall are deep (I). All mountains are tall + Some tall are rivers = no definite mountains-rivers conclusion, so no mountains-deep conclusion (II fails).

### Q27. (b) Only II follows.
Some+Some = no red-green conclusion (I fails). Some blue are green -> Some green are blue (II, conversion).

### Q28. (a) Only I follows.
All P are Q + All Q are R = All P are R (I). Some R are S gives no P-S conclusion (II fails).

### Q29. (b) Only II follows.
No sun is moon + All moons are stars: suns and stars may overlap (I fails). All moons are stars -> Some stars are moons (II).

### Q30. (d) All follow.

### Q31. (c) Both follow.

### Q32. (a) Only I follows.

### Q33. (d) Neither follows.

### Q34. (c) Both follow.

### Q35. (d) All follow.

### Q36. (a) Only I follows.

### Q37. (c) Both follow.

### Q38. (a) Only I follows.

### Q39. (b) Only II follows.
All+Some: no definite elephant-grey conclusion (I fails). Some big are grey + No grey is white = Some big are not white (II).

### Q40. (c) Either I or II follows.
Some A are B + All B are C = Some A are C (definite). But "All A are C" (I) and "Some A are not C" (II) -- I is not definite, II is not definite. They form an A/O complementary pair. Either I or II.

### Q41. (a) Only I follows.
All+All: All squares are parallelograms (I). Some parallelograms are rhombuses + All squares are parallelograms: no definite squares-rhombuses link (II fails).

### Q42. (b) Only II follows.
No computer is phone + All phones are smart: computers and smart may overlap (I fails). Phones are smart and not computers -> Some smart are not computers (II).

### Q43. (a) Only I follows.
Some drugs are chemicals + All chemicals are harmful = Some drugs are harmful (I). All medicines are drugs + Some drugs are harmful: no definite medicine-harmful link (II fails).

### Q44. (a) Only I follows.
Some singers are dancers + All dancers are artists = Some singers are artists (I). Some actors are singers + Some singers are dancers: Some+Some = no definite actor-artist link (II fails).

### Q45. (b) I and III follow.
All roses are jasmine + No jasmine is lily = No rose is lily (I). All roses are jasmine -> Some jasmine are roses (III). Some lilies are lotus: no definite jasmine-lotus conclusion but "Some lotus are not jasmine" needs testing. Lilies and jasmine are separate (No jasmine is lily), lotus may or may not be jasmine, so we can't definitively say some lotus are not jasmine. II does not necessarily follow.
