## How to Use This Material

1. **First pass:** Answer each question in your head or on paper without looking at notes.
2. **Second pass:** For questions you could not answer, re-read the relevant subtopic file, then try again.
3. **Design questions:** Sketch diagrams on paper. Practice explaining your design to an imaginary interviewer.
4. **Scenario questions:** There is often no single right answer. Focus on trade-offs and justification.
5. **Use the answer hints** at the bottom only after attempting the question yourself.

## A. Monolithic vs Microservices (6.1.a)

### Conceptual

1. Define a monolithic architecture in your own words. What are its three defining characteristics?

2. List four advantages of a monolithic architecture.

3. List four disadvantages of a monolithic architecture.

4. What is the fundamental difference between how modules communicate in a monolith versus how services communicate in microservices?

5. Explain Conway's Law and how it relates to the choice between monolith and microservices.

6. What is a "distributed monolith"? How does it differ from a true microservices architecture?

7. What is a modular monolith? How does it differ from both a traditional monolith and microservices?

### Design

8. You have a monolithic Express app with user management, product catalog, and order processing. Sketch how you would restructure this as microservices. What are the service boundaries? What database does each service own?

9. Describe the Strangler Fig Pattern. For an e-commerce monolith, which module would you extract first? Justify your choice.

10. Design a migration plan for moving a monolithic blog platform (users, posts, comments, notifications) to microservices. Identify the order of extraction and explain your reasoning.

### Scenario

11. A startup has a monolith with 200,000 lines of code, 3 developers, and 500 daily users. A new CTO proposes rewriting as microservices. What is your recommendation and why?

12. Netflix and Etsy both serve millions of users. Netflix uses microservices; Etsy uses a monolith. Explain how both can be successful with opposite architectural choices.

## B. When Microservices Make Sense (6.1.b)

### Conceptual

13. List five components of the "microservices premium" (operational overhead).

14. What team size threshold generally suggests microservices become worthwhile? Why?

15. Explain the YAGNI principle in the context of microservices.

16. What is "resume-driven development" and why is it a bad reason to adopt microservices?

17. Why are microservices particularly harmful for early-stage startups?

### Design

18. Using the decision matrix from 6.1.b (team size, deploy frequency, domain complexity, scaling variance, uptime, tech diversity, regulatory needs), score the following company and recommend an architecture:
    - 8 engineers
    - Deploy once a week
    - Single product (SaaS dashboard)
    - Uniform traffic patterns
    - 99.5% uptime target
    - All JavaScript/TypeScript
    - No regulatory isolation needed

19. Same exercise for:
    - 40 engineers across 6 teams
    - Want to deploy multiple times per day
    - 5 distinct business domains (marketplace, payments, logistics, analytics, messaging)
    - Highly variable traffic (logistics spikes during holidays)
    - 99.99% uptime target
    - Need Python for ML, Java for payments, Node for real-time
    - PCI-DSS compliance for payment data

### Scenario

20. Your company has 12 engineers and a monolith. Deployments are painful (1-2 per week), and one team frequently blocks another. The VP of Engineering wants to move to microservices. What intermediate step would you suggest before going full microservices?

21. A company successfully migrated to microservices 2 years ago. They recently downsized from 50 to 15 engineers. What architectural changes should they consider?

22. Describe a scenario where a company adopted microservices, failed, and returned to a monolith. What went wrong?

## C. Service Boundaries (6.1.c)

### Conceptual

23. Define "bounded context" in Domain-Driven Design. Why is it the natural unit for microservice decomposition?

24. What is "ubiquitous language"? Why does the word "Product" mean different things in a Catalog context versus a Shipping context?

25. Explain the difference between an Entity and a Value Object with examples.

26. What is an Aggregate in DDD? Why is it important for microservice boundaries?

27. What is the Anti-Corruption Layer pattern? When would you use it?

28. Name three common mistakes when defining service boundaries.

### Design

29. Decompose a ride-sharing application (like Uber) into bounded contexts. For each context, list the key entities and 2-3 core operations.

30. Decompose a streaming music platform (like Spotify) into bounded contexts. Identify at least 5 services.

31. A team proposes the following microservices for a hospital management system: CreatePatient, ReadPatient, UpdatePatient, DeletePatient, CreateAppointment, ReadAppointment. What is wrong with this decomposition? Propose a better one.

32. Draw a context map for an e-commerce system showing the relationships between Catalog, Order, Payment, and Shipping contexts. Identify which relationships are Customer-Supplier, Conformist, or Anti-Corruption Layer.

### Scenario

33. Two teams disagree about whether "reviews" belongs in the Catalog Service or a separate Reviews Service. What questions would you ask to resolve this?

34. Your Order Service makes 15 API calls to the Inventory Service per order. A colleague suggests they should be merged. Do you agree? What factors would influence your decision?

35. The Marketing team wants to add promotional pricing that affects products across Catalog, Orders, and Checkout. How do you handle this without violating service boundaries?

## D. Database per Service (6.1.d)

### Conceptual

36. Why do shared databases break microservices? Give three specific problems.

37. Explain the difference between strong consistency and eventual consistency. Give an example of each in an e-commerce system.

38. What is the Saga pattern? How does it replace ACID transactions in microservices?

39. Explain the difference between a choreography-based saga and an orchestration-based saga. When would you use each?

40. What is a compensating transaction? Give an example.

41. What is the API Composition pattern? When is it appropriate?

42. What is CQRS? When should you use it and when should you avoid it?

### Design

43. Design a saga for a travel booking system that books a flight, a hotel, and a rental car. Show the happy path and the compensation path if the hotel booking fails.

44. You have an Order Service, Inventory Service, and Payment Service. Design the database schema for each service's database. What data does each service own? What data is duplicated?

45. A product manager wants a dashboard that shows "top 10 products by revenue this month." Revenue data is in the Order Service. Product names are in the Catalog Service. Design two approaches: API Composition and CQRS. Compare them.

### Scenario

46. An engineer proposes that the Order Service should read directly from the User database "just for performance." What do you say?

47. Your saga has a step where the Payment Service charges a credit card. The charge succeeds, but the message acknowledging success is lost due to a network partition. What happens? How do you prevent double-charging?

48. The read-side projection in your CQRS system is 30 seconds behind the write-side. A customer places an order and immediately checks their order history -- the order is missing. How do you handle this UX problem?

## E. Service Communication Patterns (6.1.e)

### Conceptual

49. Compare synchronous and asynchronous communication in microservices. List three differences.

50. What is temporal coupling? How does asynchronous communication eliminate it?

51. Explain the circuit breaker pattern. Why is it essential in microservices?

52. What is the difference between a message queue (point-to-point) and pub/sub (one-to-many)?

53. Compare RabbitMQ and Kafka. When would you choose each?

54. What does "idempotent" mean? Why is idempotency critical for message consumers?

55. Explain the difference between choreography and orchestration for saga coordination.

### Design

56. Design the communication patterns for a food delivery app. The flow is: customer places order, restaurant confirms, driver is assigned, driver picks up food, driver delivers, customer rates. Which steps are synchronous? Which are asynchronous?

57. Your system has the following call chain: API Gateway -> Order Service -> Inventory Service -> Pricing Service. Each call takes 100ms plus 10ms network overhead. What is the total latency? Propose two strategies to reduce it below 150ms.

58. Design an event-driven notification system. When a user signs up, three things should happen: send a welcome email, create a default profile, and log an analytics event. Show the event flow using pub/sub.

### Scenario

59. Your Order Service calls the User Service synchronously. The User Service has a 1% error rate. Your Order Service processes 10,000 orders per day. How many orders fail due to User Service errors? What would you do to reduce this?

60. A developer puts all inter-service communication through Kafka, including simple "get user by ID" queries. The response time for these queries is 500ms (Kafka consumer lag). What is wrong with this approach? What would you recommend?

61. Your system uses choreography-based sagas. There are now 8 services in the saga flow. Debugging a failed order requires checking logs in all 8 services. What would you change?

62. The message broker goes down for 5 minutes. What happens to your system in: (a) a synchronous REST architecture, (b) an asynchronous event-driven architecture?
